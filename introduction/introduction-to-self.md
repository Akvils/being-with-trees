# ☝️ Introduction to Self

## The Human Being

We become like who we spend most of our time with. Being with trees will make us more and more like trees. There are some objective markers of this. Like being in a constant state of awareness, a state of being connected with totality, breathing in and out, taking only what is necessary, giving more than taking, generating abundant energy, always dancing and celebrating, being true to its nature, having deep roots equal to the branches above and all the other good ol redeeming qualities.

Before we can understand what it means to be a tree, we have to understand something about ourselves.

We tend to assume that we are conscious. We wake up in the morning, go to work, make decisions, speak to people, think about the future, remember the past, and move through the world as though there is a single person inside us directing all of this.

But if we look closely, this is not quite what is happening.

Most of the time, we are asleep. Not asleep in the ordinary sense of the word, but asleep to ourselves. We live mechanically. Thoughts appear, emotions arise, reactions happen, desires come and go, and we follow them. Something happens outside us, and something happens inside us in response. We call this response “my decision,” “my feeling,” or “what I wanted.”

But how much of it did we actually choose?

If we observe ourselves honestly, we begin to notice something uncomfortable.

There is not one “I” inside us. There are many.

One “I” wants to work. Another wants to rest. One wants to change. Another wants to remain exactly as it is. One wants to be kind. Another becomes angry. One makes a promise in the morning, and another breaks it by evening.

Each one speaks as though it is the whole person. For a moment, one of these “I”s is in control, and we say, “I.” Then another takes over, and it says “I.” We rarely notice the change.

### Awakening

The possibility of awakening begins when we recognize that we are asleep. If we already believed ourselves to be fully conscious, there would be no need to do any work for it. But consciousness is not simply something we have. It is something that can be developed. This requires effort.

We cannot drift into consciousness. We cannot simply decide that from tomorrow we will be more aware and expect that decision to transform us. The mechanical forces that already operate in us are stronger than our ordinary intentions.

There must be a method. There must be a deliberate effort to see ourselves as we actually are. This is what is meant by the Work. The Work begins with something very simple.

Observe yourself.

### Self-Observation

Begin to watch. Watch your thoughts, emotions and reactions. Notice what happens when someone criticizes you, praises you, ignores you or refuses to give you what you want. Notice how quickly you justify yourself, blame someone else or imagine what another person is thinking.

The instruction is not to change what you see or condemn it, but simply to see.

This is harder than it sounds because normally we do not observe anger; we become angry. We do not observe fear; we become afraid. We do not observe a thought; we follow it.

Self-observation creates a separation: something is happening in me, and I can see it happening. That distance is the beginning of freedom. Until we can see the machine, we cannot work on the machine.

### The Human Machine

When we observe ourselves more carefully, we begin to see that we function through different parts of ourselves.

There is the intellectual part, through which we think, compare, analyse, remember and imagine.

There is the emotional part, through which we feel attraction, fear, love, anger, enthusiasm, jealousy and sadness.

There is the moving and instinctive part, through which the body moves, reacts, maintains itself and carries out countless processes without conscious direction.

And there is the sexual centre, which carries an enormous amount of energy and has functions far beyond what we ordinarily associate with sexuality.

These centres are not inherently a problem.

The problem is that they are often mixed, confused and misused.

We try to think with emotion and feel with thought. We use intellectual energy to repeat emotional stories. We exhaust ourselves through unnecessary movement, unnecessary speech and unnecessary imagination. We use powerful energies for purposes that do not lead anywhere.

We are a machine with enormous potential, but we do not know how the machine works. And because we do not know how it works, we mistake its activity for ourselves.

### Mechanicalness and the Illusion of Will

Look at an ordinary day. You reach for the phone, remember something and become annoyed, hear someone say something and react, see something you want and begin imagining having it. You form an opinion before you have consciously decided to form one. Much of this has happened thousands of times before.

We call it doing, but often things are simply happening through us. This is mechanicalness.

The first shock is not merely understanding this intellectually. It is catching yourself in the act: promising yourself that you will not react and then watching yourself react, deciding that you will change and then watching another "I" take over, saying something and realizing immediately afterwards that you did not really choose to say it.

Then we begin to see the problem with our idea of will. Which "I" is choosing?

The "I" that made the decision yesterday may not be present today. One part wants discipline while another wants comfort; one wants truth while another wants approval. Until there is some degree of unity, what we call will is often only the temporary victory of one "I" over another.

Real will requires a centre from which we can actually act.

### False Personality and Essence

As observation deepens, we begin to distinguish between what we have acquired and what is more fundamental in us.

We learn how to behave, what kind of person we are supposed to be, what to show and what to hide. We build an identity from family, culture, education, success, failure, approval and fear. This is a false personality.

It is not necessarily false in the sense of being deliberately dishonest. It is false because we mistake what we have acquired for what we are.

Underneath it is essence, what is more truly our own.

We can experience ourselves as three people. There is the person we present to the world, the person we are afraid we might be underneath that presentation, with thoughts, impulses and desires we would rather not admit, and beneath both is the possibility of the self we actually are.

The conflict between the first two produces a sense of incompleteness. We feel something is missing, and so we look outside ourselves for completion.

### Gratification and Satisfaction

We look for completion in things like money, beauty, recognition, possessions, experiences, status, pleasure etc. There is nothing inherently wrong with any of these things. The problem is expecting them to complete us. But these things are only gratifying. They don’t satisfy this endless loop of wanting more, better or different. &#x20;

Gratification can always demand another object, something better, newer, larger or more exciting. Satisfaction is different. It is the experience of being whole and complete.

It cannot be brought in from outside. It is not something another person can give us, nor something we can permanently acquire. The experience of the self as the self is intrinsically satisfying.

We do not have to manufacture this state. We have to remove what prevents us from recognizing it.

### The School and the Laws

This is why the Work cannot simply be done through good intentions. Left alone, the machine turns everything, even the Work itself, into another form of sleep. We can read about consciousness without becoming conscious, talk about observation without observing ourselves, and even build a spiritual identity around the idea that we are becoming more awake.

Transformation needs an environment that provides guidance, method and friction. Friction is especially valuable because resistance reveals us. When someone disagrees with us, ignores us or prevents us from getting what we want, the machine becomes visible. What we normally call an obstacle can therefore become material for the Work.

There are also laws governing how processes develop.

The Law of Three describes the interaction of three forces: active, passive and neutralizing. Nothing develops simply because one thing pushes against another; a third force is required to create a new relationship.

The Law of Seven, or the law of octaves, shows why development does not proceed evenly. We begin with energy and intention, but momentum changes. Processes encounter intervals and begin to deviate. This is why resolutions fade, practices are abandoned and good intentions collapse.

At certain points, a conscious intervention is required: a shock. Without it, the process follows its mechanical course. In this context it will be useful to introduce shock to complete the program as you might find it difficult to complete the whole thing due to resistance or some other factors.

### Self-Remembering

One of the central practices of the Work is self-remembering.

Normally, our attention is absorbed by whatever is happening. We speak and become absorbed in speaking; we walk and become absorbed in walking; we become angry and become absorbed in the anger. Self-remembering introduces another dimension: I am here, and I know that I am here.

I am doing something while, at the same time, I am aware of myself doing it.

This is not thinking about yourself or analysing yourself. It is a direct experience of being present to yourself while life is happening. For a moment, there is both the experience and the one experiencing it, and that changes the quality of attention.

### Energy

Consciousness requires energy, yet we constantly spend energy mechanically through unnecessary talking, emotional reactions, imagination, anxiety, fantasy, self-importance and the endless rehearsal of situations that are not actually happening.

We leak energy through identification. The aim is not repression but understanding. What is unconscious can be seen; what is seen can be conserved; what is conserved can potentially be transformed.

The sexual centre is particularly important because it contains a great deal of energy. When this energy is used mechanically, it is dissipated. When understood and integrated, it can support the whole organism.

### Friction and Conscious Suffering

This is why the Work is not always comfortable.

If the aim were simply to feel better, we could develop many strategies for avoiding ourselves. The aim is to become conscious, which means seeing what we normally hide: our vanity, fear, resentment, need for approval, laziness, dishonesty, imagination and desire to appear better than we are.

This can produce suffering, but there is a difference between suffering unconsciously and suffering consciously. Unconscious suffering simply happens to us. Conscious suffering is the willingness to remain present with something we would normally escape.

Friction exposes the machine, and once the machine is seen, we have a choice.

### A Centre of Gravity

If the Work becomes important enough, something begins to stabilize. Our attention is no longer completely scattered among every passing desire, and we begin to develop a centre of gravity.

We start to value consciousness more than comfort. We see the uselessness of certain mechanical patterns and recognize how much of our life has been spent asleep.

The Work is no longer something we occasionally remember to do. It begins to organize our life. We lose ourselves and remember, again and again, and this repeated return gradually creates direction.

### Higher Possibilities

The human being contains possibilities that are normally inaccessible.

The higher emotional and higher intellectual centres already exist, but ordinary consciousness does not give us access to them. Our ordinary state is too noisy; thought, emotion, imagination and identification occupy the whole field.

As this noise becomes quieter, another quality of experience becomes possible. This is not simply becoming more attentive or more productive. It is a change in the quality of being.

### Four States of Consciousness

There are four states traditionally described in the Work.

The first is ordinary sleep. The second is what we normally call waking life. But from this perspective, ordinary waking life is still a form of sleep. We move, work, desire, react and think, but remain largely identified with whatever is happening inside us.

The third is self-consciousness: consciousness of ourselves as well as of what we are experiencing.

The fourth is objective consciousness: a state in which reality can be perceived without the ordinary distortions of mechanical personality.

Most people move between the first two states throughout their lives. The possibility of the third changes everything.

### Becoming Real

The aim is not to become a better version of our personality, construct a more impressive spiritual identity or appear conscious. The aim is to become conscious, to become unified and to develop real will: to move from being a collection of competing "I"s toward becoming someone who can say I and mean one thing.

This is what it means to become a real integrated human being.

And this is where the trees enter. The understanding of trees being deities and forests being sacred is an ancient one. All these ideas can be integrated very effectively in natural settings among trees. Combining it with the energetic journeys and meditations, gives us an excellent ecosystem to make an explosion of consciousness and drop the psychosomatic diseases we inherit living in the society. That’s the journey we’re going to start.
