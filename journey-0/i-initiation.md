# 🏞️ I: Initiation

Every journey begins with intention. And here, we make the first distinction: when we step outside our home, where are we truly going?

We can enter the human world, a place bound by rules, complexity, programs, and noise. Here we often feel disconnected, heavy, and judged. This is the 3D material world. Or we can choose to enter the natural world,  where there are trees, birds, rivers, and skies that carry no judgment, no programs, only presence. This is the astral, heart-centred world. After all, it is green.

This distinction first became clear to me many years ago in India, during a time of deep confusion. I was living in a university hostel, going through what I now recognise as a spiritual awakening. Outside my window stood a mango tree. I used to sit with it, silently, seeking some kind of relief.

On 12 December 2012 (12:12:12), the day Mayans said the world would end, it truly did for me. That evening, there was a festival on campus. I went outside and watched the people around me. They were shouting, laughing, crying, all acting through their programs, caught in their egos. From my state of emptiness and questioning, I could see how chaotic and heavy it all was.

I felt no desire to join them. Instead, I returned to my mango tree. And there I realised: the human world felt exhausting, almost toxic, while the natural world felt calm, connected, and home.

That was the first time I made the choice: not to live in the heavy human world, but to live in the natural world. Among the trees, among the birds, where things simply are.

This is where the journey begins, with intention and with the choice of which world to enter. When you make that choice, you move from the material world to the ethereal one.

## Walking Meditation

Cultivating awareness through movement

Walking meditation is a practice of bringing your full attention to the simple act of walking. Rather than walking to reach a destination, we walk to become aware of each movement as it unfolds.

### The Practice

#### 1. Begin by standing still

Find a quiet, safe place where you can walk slowly without interruption. If possible, choose a natural setting near trees.

Stand still and bring your attention to your body. Become aware of yourself standing, of the ground beneath your feet, and of the space around you.

Remain still for a few moments, allowing your attention to settle.

#### 2. Walk with awareness

Begin walking very slowly.

Keep your attention on your feet. Observe each movement as it happens:

* Become aware of your foot lifting from the ground.
* Notice it moving through the air.
* Become aware of it descending and making contact with the ground.

Observe each step from beginning to end. Do not walk mechanically or allow your mind to wander ahead. Your task is simply to remain aware of the movement taking place now.

Continue walking slowly, maintaining your attention on your feet.

#### 3. Pause and become aware of standing

When you reach an obstacle or the end of your walking path, stop.

Do not turn immediately. Remain standing still for a count of ten.

During this pause, become fully aware that you are standing. Notice the stillness of your body and the contact between your feet and the ground.

Do not rush to begin the next movement. First, be aware of where you are.

#### 4. Turn with awareness

Before turning, become aware that you are about to change direction.

Bring your attention to the shoulder on the side toward which you intend to turn. Become aware of the intention to turn before the movement begins.

Now turn around slowly, taking a count of six to complete the turn. Remain aware of the movement of your body throughout the turn.

Once you have turned, pause briefly, become aware of yourself standing, and then begin walking again.

#### 5. Continue the practice

Repeat this sequence for one hour:

Walk slowly, keeping your attention on each step. When you reach an obstacle, stop and remain aware of standing for a count of ten. Bring your attention to the shoulder in the direction of the turn, turn slowly over a count of six, and resume walking.

Whenever you notice that your attention has wandered, gently bring it back to the movement of your feet.

Do not try to create a particular experience or feeling. The practice is to remain aware of each movement as it happens.

#### Integration

After one hour, stop walking and find a place to sit comfortably.

Remain quiet for several minutes. Allow the body to settle and observe your present state.

And continue with the next process.
